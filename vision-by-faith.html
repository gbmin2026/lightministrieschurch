Vision by Faith

By Pastor Gary Bailey · Light Ministries Church, Lewistown, PA

By Gary Bailey

Proverbs 29 says, “Where there is no vision, the people perish.” That is true for a person, a family, and a church. Without vision, life becomes only routine. We get up, go to work, pay bills, solve problems, and move through another week. But God did not save us merely so we could survive. He saved us to know Him, serve Him and help others find life in Christ.

Vision is more than eyesight. It is seeing by faith what God has called us to be and do. A church gathers to worship God, but worship is not the only reason we meet. We gather for fellowship, for the teaching of the Word, for prayer, for discipleship, and for strength to carry the gospel beyond the walls of the church.

That last part is easy to forget. We can become so focused on what happens inside the building that we forget Jesus came “to seek and to save that which was lost.” If we searched every room of a church building looking for lost people, we might not find many. The lost are usually outside the walls. They are in neighborhoods, workplaces, stores, schools, families, and conversations.

That does not make church less important. It makes church even more important. The gathered church is where believers are fed, corrected, encouraged, and stirred again with purpose. We come in to worship, hear the Word, and be strengthened in faith. Then we go out to love people, witness, serve, invite, and pray. A healthy church is not a hiding place from the world. It is a place where God prepares His people to reach the world.

Every Christian should ask, “What do I see for my life in God?” It is good to have goals for education, work, finances, and family. But what about your Christian life? Do you see yourself growing in the Word? Do you see yourself becoming a disciple, not merely an attendee? Do you see yourself helping someone else know Jesus? Do you see your church as a place where God can use you?

There are different levels of church life. Some remain on the edges, receiving when they can. Others move closer and become more consistent. Then there are disciples, those who say, “I want to be fruitful. I want to apply what I learn. I want to help carry the purpose of God.” Every church needs people at all stages, but every believer should keep growing.

One great danger to vision is spiritual blindness. The Bible says the god of this world blinds the minds of those who do not believe, lest the gospel of Christ should shine unto them. Many people are not rejecting Christ because they have carefully understood Him. They simply cannot see. That is why we pray for eyes to be opened, share the gospel patiently, and invite people into the life of the church.

But Christians must also guard against blindness. We can lose sight through worry, offense, pride, apathy, or the cares of life. Bills, trouble, disappointment, or criticism can choke the Word and make us forget why we are here. Even believers can become lukewarm, thinking they need nothing, when in truth they need fresh fire, humility, and vision.

Love helps keep our eyes clear. Scripture says that whoever hates his brother walks in darkness. A church cannot fulfill its vision while filled with strife, suspicion, and faultfinding. We must learn to be patient with one another, forgive one another, and believe the best as much as we can. Walking in love is not weakness. Sometimes it is one of the strongest things a Christian will ever do.

Faith is the key to lasting vision. “We walk by faith, not by sight.” The heroes of faith in Hebrews saw something beyond their present circumstances. Noah, Abraham, Moses, Rahab, David, and the prophets all moved because faith gave them vision. They endured hardship because they trusted the promise of God more than the difficulty in front of them.

The same must be true of us. We need to see more than empty seats; we need to see people God loves and wants to save. We need to see more than another Sunday service; we need to see an opportunity for people to be helped, taught, encouraged, and changed by the Word of God. We need to see more than our own comfort and remember that eternity is real.

So do not lose your vision. Come. Hear the Word consistently. Pray for the lost. Invite someone to church. Walk in love. Serve where you can. Trust God beyond the present moment, even when the results are not yet visible. Faith keeps seeing what God has promised and keeps walking with Him until the harvest comes.
