Right and Wrong Thinking

By Pastor Gary Bailey · Light Ministries Church, Lewistown, PA

By Gary Bailey

The direction of a life is often determined long before a person takes a step. It begins in the thoughts. What we allow into our minds shapes what we believe, what we say, and eventually where we go. That is why Romans 12 gives such a clear instruction: "Be not conformed to this world: but be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind."

Renewing the mind is not a small matter. It is part of how God changes a person from the inside out. The world is constantly pressing its ideas, fears, desires, and philosophies upon us. Some of those ideas sound reasonable. Some may even contain a little truth. But the Christian is called to measure life by something higher than opinion, culture, or popular sayings. We are called to build our lives on the Word of God.

Paul first says to present our bodies a living sacrifice. That matters because our minds are affected by where our bodies go. If we place ourselves before the Word of God, in church, in Scripture, in prayer, and in fellowship, we are giving God access to renew us. If we constantly place ourselves before voices that mock faith, stir fear, excuse sin, or promote pride, those voices will influence us too.

This is one reason regular church attendance is so important. We do not gather merely out of habit. We gather because we need the Word preached, worship shared, faith strengthened, and our thoughts brought back into line with God's truth. A person may say, "I can read my Bible on my own," and certainly every believer should. But God also ordained fellowship. The normal Christian life includes gathering with other believers, hearing the Word, and being part of something larger than ourselves.

Still, Sunday is not meant to be the only time we receive spiritual food. A mature believer learns to feed on God's Word daily. Even fifteen minutes in Scripture can help set the course of a day. The mind does not stay renewed automatically. Someone once said the mind stays renewed about as long as the hair stays combed. It takes attention. It takes maintenance. When our thinking gets messy, fearful, proud, or discouraged, we go back to the Word and let God comb things out again.

Right thinking produces right believing. Right believing produces right speaking. Jesus said a person will have what he says when he believes in his heart. That is why we must be careful not to let defeat, bitterness, fear, or unbelief become the language of our lives. God does not call His children failures. He calls believers new creatures in Christ. He says they are forgiven, loved, redeemed, and created for good works.

This does not mean Christians deny trouble. Pain, temptation, sickness, and pressure are real. But the believer must decide what voice will be final. Will we believe the fear that says, "You will never make it," or the promise of God that says He is with us? Will we think continually on what is wrong, or will we think on what is true, honest, just, pure, lovely, and of good report?

Right thinking also affects how we see others. Love "thinketh no evil." It does not keep searching for the worst possible explanation. In families, marriages, churches, and friendships, wrong thinking can turn small offenses into large divisions. But when the Word renews our minds, we learn humility. We remember that all of us need mercy. We stop thinking of ourselves more highly than we ought to think and begin to walk in patience and grace.

For the person who is not yet a Christian, this is a gracious invitation. God is not asking you to empty your mind or follow blind religion. He invites you to hear His Word, consider Christ, and discover that the gospel is true. Heaven is real. Forgiveness is available. Jesus came not to destroy your life, but to save it.

For the believer, this is a call to stay strong and courageous. Joshua was told to meditate on the law of God day and night, not so he could merely know more, but so he could walk wisely and prosper in the will of God. The same principle remains: the Word we hear, receive, believe, and obey shapes the life we live.

So choose your thoughts carefully. Bring your body where your mind can be renewed. Sit under the Word consistently. Open the Bible daily. Stay in fellowship with the church. Refuse the lies of the enemy, and hold fast to the truth of God. A renewed mind leads to a steadier heart, a stronger faith, and a life that points others to Christ.
