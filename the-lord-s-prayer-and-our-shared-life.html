The Lord’s Prayer and Our Shared Life

By Pastor Gary Bailey · Light Ministries Church, Lewistown, PA

By Gary Bailey

Many people know the words of the Lord’s Prayer. Some learned them as children. Some have heard them in church services, funerals, weddings, or family gatherings. But the prayer Jesus gave us was never meant to be only a religious recitation. It is a pattern for life. It teaches us how to approach God, trust Him, forgive others, and live as part of a spiritual family.

In Matthew 6, Jesus said, “After this manner therefore pray ye: Our Father which art in heaven, Hallowed be thy name.” The first word is important: “Our.” Prayer begins with relationship. God is not merely a distant power. He is Father. Yet Jesus did not teach us to pray, “My Father” only, but “Our Father.” The Christian life was never designed to be lived alone.

This is one reason the church matters so much. When believers gather on the first day of the week, as the church has done since the days of the apostles, we come to worship, hear the Word, pray, and be strengthened for the week ahead. Sunday worship is not just a tradition. It is spiritual food. It is a way of giving God the first part of our week and saying, “Lord, You are first in my life.”

Of course, no Christian should depend only on what is preached on Sunday. A mature believer learns to feed himself or herself daily on the Word of God. Even one verse read with faith can steady the soul. A few sincere moments in prayer can turn the direction of a day. But the private life and the gathered life belong together. We need the secret place with God, and we need the fellowship of the saints.

Jesus warned against praying to be seen of men. True prayer is not performance. It begins in the hidden place, where no one is applauding and no one is impressed. “Thy Father which seeth in secret shall reward thee openly.” Much of the blessing people see openly often began secretly: a prayer no one heard, a gift no one announced, an act of obedience no one noticed.

That kind of secret faithfulness shapes a person. It teaches us to listen to the conscience, obey the still small voice of God, and do the simple good before us. Sometimes it may be as ordinary as picking up trash, helping someone in need, giving quietly, or making a wrong right. God sees those things. He works through people willing to obey Him in small matters.

The Lord’s Prayer also teaches us what to desire: “Thy kingdom come. Thy will be done in earth, as it is in heaven.” That is more than a sentence to repeat. It is a surrender. It means we want God’s will in our homes, marriages, finances, church, and community. It means we want people healed, helped, forgiven, saved, restored, and brought into the knowledge of Christ.

A healthy church carries that burden together. We do not gather only for ourselves. We gather so we can be strengthened and sent back into the world with grace, truth, and love. There are people in every town who need the gospel. Some are lonely. Some are bound by habits they cannot break. Some are discouraged. Some have never really heard that God is not their enemy, but their answer.

Jesus also taught us to pray, “Give us this day our daily bread.” God cares about daily needs. He knows what we need before we ask. Peace, provision, wisdom, courage, forgiveness, and help are all found in Him. Like a good father caring for his children, the Lord is attentive to those who trust Him.

Then Jesus brings the prayer close to home: “Forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors.” He ends the passage by emphasizing forgiveness again. That should get our attention. The life of prayer cannot be separated from the life of love. We cannot truly walk with God while refusing to forgive people. In families, marriages, churches, and friendships, offenses will come. People will miss it. We will miss it too. The mature believer learns to go, speak, forgive, and make things right.

That is another blessing of church life. Fellowship gives us the opportunity to practice love, patience, humility, and forgiveness. It is not always easy, but it is deeply Christian. God never designed us to be spiritual hermits. He designed us to grow together.

So pray the Lord’s Prayer, but do more than recite it. Let it form your life. Put God first. Hear His Word consistently. Feed on Scripture daily. Gather with His people faithfully. Forgive freely. Listen for His voice. And as you do, you will find that the Father who sees in secret still rewards openly.
