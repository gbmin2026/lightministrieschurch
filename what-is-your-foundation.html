What Is Your Foundation?

By Pastor Gary Bailey · Light Ministries Church, Lewistown, PA

In a world of shifting opinions, competing religions, and constant debate, one question still matters more than ever: What is your foundation?

The Bible is a large and layered book. A person could study it for a lifetime and continue discovering new depths. There are teachings about faith, prayer, healing, morality, prophecy, and hope. Yet beneath all those themes is one central message holding everything together.

The foundation is not a denomination. It is not a church program. It is not a personality or a movement. The foundation is a person — Jesus Christ.

The Apostle Paul wrote in 1 Corinthians 3:11 (KJV), “For other foundation can no man lay than that is laid, which is Jesus Christ.” That statement is simple, yet it carries enormous weight. Every teaching, every sermon, every spiritual insight must ultimately rest on Him.

In our region, we are blessed with a rich religious heritage. Amish, Mennonite, Baptist, Methodist, Catholic, Pentecostal, and others worship faithfully in their communities. Yet beneath all traditions stands one shared question: Who is Jesus to you?

The Gospel of Matthew begins, “The book of the generation of Jesus Christ” (Matthew 1:1, KJV). The story is not primarily about religious systems — it is about Christ Himself. The angel told Joseph concerning Mary’s child, “Thou shalt call his name Jesus: for he shall save his people from their sins” (Matthew 1:21, KJV).

That is the heart of Christianity — not ceremony, not ritual, not even moral improvement. Salvation.

The Bible teaches that all have sinned and come short of the glory of God. We know this instinctively. No one has lived perfectly. Sin creates separation between humanity and God. That separation is what Scripture calls death.

Jesus came to bridge that separation.

John declared, “Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world” (John 1:29, KJV). Not just one group. Not one denomination. The sin of the world.

If the foundation is weak, everything built upon it eventually cracks. If the foundation is secure, the structure can endure storms.

Jesus said, “All power is given unto me in heaven and in earth” (Matthew 28:18, KJV). That could sound intimidating — until we remember what Scripture also says: “God is love” (1 John 4:8, KJV). The power of Christ is not rooted in tyranny. It is rooted in love. “For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son” (John 3:16, KJV).

Power without love destroys. Power grounded in love rescues. Human leaders may hold authority, but none holds ultimate power over the soul. Jesus told Pilate, “Thou couldest have no power at all against me, except it were given thee from above” (John 19:11, KJV).

Governments matter. Community matters. Authority matters. But above all stands Christ.

For those who feel overlooked, the story of Zacchaeus is comforting. He was “little of stature” (Luke 19:3, KJV), unable to see over the crowd. Yet when he sought Jesus, he discovered Jesus was seeking him. When you draw near to God, He draws near to you.

For those uncertain about eternity, Jesus said plainly, “I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me” (John 14:6, KJV). That statement is exclusive, but it is also gracious. The way is not hidden. It is revealed.

Salvation is not inherited like farmland or family tradition. Each person must respond personally. “Whosoever shall confess that Jesus is the Son of God, God dwelleth in him, and he in God” (1 John 4:15, KJV).

Storms will come — illness, loss, uncertainty, cultural change. Foundations are tested in storms. If your hope rests only in human strength, it may falter. But if it rests in Christ, it will stand.

Paul wrote, “For we preach not ourselves, but Christ Jesus the Lord” (2 Corinthians 4:5, KJV). That remains the message today.

So I leave you with this simple question: Is Jesus Christ your foundation? Not merely your tradition. Not merely your upbringing. Not merely your church attendance. But your personal foundation.

If you have never trusted Him personally, you can. Right where you are, you can call upon Him. Confess that He is the Son of God. Believe that He died for your sins and rose again. Ask Him to forgive you and to become Lord of your life. The Bible says, “Whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved” (Romans 10:13, KJV).

He is still the Savior of the world. And He is still receiving all who come to Him.
