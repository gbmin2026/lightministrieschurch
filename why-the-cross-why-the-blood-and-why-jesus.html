Why the Cross, Why the Blood and Why Jesus?

By Pastor Gary Bailey · Light Ministries Church, Lewistown, PA

As Easter approaches, many people think about the resurrection of Jesus Christ. We should. The resurrection is the triumphant declaration that Jesus is alive, that death has been conquered, and that salvation is still available to everyone who believes.

Before Easter morning, Jesus had to die. Before the empty tomb, there was the cross. Before the resurrection, there was the shedding of blood. And for many people, that raises the question: Why did any of this happen?

Why the cross? Why the blood? Why Jesus?

These questions go to the heart of the Christian faith.

The real problem is not simply that people make mistakes. The problem is sin. “For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God” (Romans 3:23). Sin separates us from a holy God. Isaiah 59:2 says, “But your iniquities have separated between you and your God.” God is loving, but He is also holy and just. He does not ignore evil nor can He excuse sin.

This means humanity has a deeper problem than bad habits or poor choices. Our sins have separated us from God. We cannot repair this rift by trying harder or becoming more religious. Romans 6:23 says, “For the wages of sin is death.” Sin carries a price.

That is why the Bible places an emphasis on blood. Hebrews 9:22 says, “without shedding of blood is no remission.” In the Old Testament, animal sacrifices were offered again and again. They were real sacrifices, but they were only temporary. They pointed towards something greater. The blood of bulls and goats could not effectively take away sin forever. It was only a shadow and a type of the perfect sacrifice yet to come.

That perfect sacrifice was Jesus Christ Himself.

Why Jesus? Because only Jesus could do what no one else could do. He was not merely a good teacher or a moral example. He was the Son of God who became man. He was fully God and fully man. If He were only a man, He could not save us. If God had not become man, He could not represent us. But in Jesus, God came near. He lived a sinless life. He had no sin, He never broke God’s law. He was, as the Scripture says, “a lamb without blemish and without spot” (1 Peter 1:19).

That is why Jesus could take our place.

Isaiah 53:6 says, “the Lord hath laid on him the iniquity of us all.” Second Corinthians 5:21 says, “For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him.” On the cross, Jesus was not dying for His own sin. He had none. He was dying for ours. He was our substitute.

The cross was not an accident. It was an exchange.

Jesus bore our sin so we could receive His righteousness. He took our judgment so we could have His mercy. He died so we could have Eternal Life.

And why the blood? Because life was being given. Jesus did not offer a symbolic gesture. He offered Himself. His blood speaks of a life poured out in love, holiness, and perfect obedience. At the cross, the justice of God and the love of God came together. God did not lower His standard. He fulfilled it Himself through His Son.

But the cross is not the end of the story. On the third day, Jesus rose again. The resurrection is Heaven’s declaration that the sacrifice was accepted. The price for sin was paid. Death was defeated. The grave did not win. Jesus is alive, and because He lives, salvation is not just a religious theory. It is a reality.

This is the Gospel: We were sinners who could not save ourselves. Jesus was our substitute in death. He shed His blood so that we could be forgiven. He rose again so we could be reconciled to God and receive the life of God in our spirits.

This salvation is not earned. It is received.

John 1:12 says, “But as many as received him, to them gave he power to become the sons of God.” Romans 10:13 says, “For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.”

That is the message of Easter. Jesus died for us and bore our sins on the cross. Jesus shed His blood for us. And Jesus rose again victorious over death, hell and the grave and He did it all for us.

The question is not whether He has done enough. He has. The question is whether we will receive what He has done.

That is why the cross matters. That is why the blood matters. That is why Jesus matters.
